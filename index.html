When does a California wildfire build its own thunderstorm?

A pyrocumulonimbus (pyroCb) is a thunderstorm made by a fire's own heat. It can loft smoke into the lower stratosphere, throw lightning and erratic winds, and make fire behaviour violent and unpredictable. This site follows 23 large California fires day by day, 491 fire days in all, and asks what separated the days that made pyroCb from the days that didn't: the atmosphere, the fire's power, the fuels, or the weather pattern.

What the colours mean

Each fire day is classed by the coldest cloud top GOES saw rising from the fire between 11 a.m. and 11 p.m.:

  • pyroCb colder than −40 °C: the plume became a thunderstorm.
  • pyroCu colder than −20 °C: a deep cumulus cap that stopped short of one.
  • ordinary storms over fire ambient thunderstorms over or beside the fire, so the satellite can't tell; left out of every score.
  • no deep plume cloud the plume's top stayed warmer than −20 °C, or it was smoke only. Not "no plume", and often not a quiet day: the biggest runs of all are here.

What we found

    The test that worked

    Tory and Kepert (2021) turned pyroCb potential into one number, the pyroCb firepower threshold (PFT): the least fire heat output the atmosphere needs before a plume can rise, condense and keep going as a thunderstorm.

    PFT (GW) ≈ 0.3 × zfc² (km) × UML (m/s) × Δθfc (K)

    zfc is how high the plume must rise before it can convect on its own, UML the wind bending it over, Δθfc the extra warmth needed to punch through stable layers. Comparing the fire's measured power with that threshold in the same hour (FP/PFT) separated pyroCb days best, in the north and the south alike.

    Two roads to a pyroCb

    Favourable air, modest fire. Dixie, Loyalton, Castle and Line made pyroCbs from 14–76 GW of fire under moist, unstable air (thresholds 6–57 GW), often with monsoon moisture moving north under a hot ridge.

    Big fire, marginal air. Creek, Carr and Sugar mostly made theirs from 110–220 GW of fire against 176–384 GW thresholds (Creek on 6 September, with a threshold of just 25 GW, was the exception).

    The biggest runs of all were neither: the 8 September 2020 offshore-wind runs (North Complex/Bear 211,000 acres, August Complex 198,000 acres) happened in air too dry for any pyroCb.

    Does it hold up?

    The first study used seven fires. Sixteen more were then added, eight in the north and eight in the south, and the rules were tried on them without refitting the cuts. Counts are days flagged out of days in each class; ordinary storms over the fire are left out.

    "North" is the Northern California GACC (ONCC) and "south" the Southern (OSCC). Forecast rules need a morning forecast from the day before, so a fire's first day is left out of them.
