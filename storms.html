Storms over fires: is there a pyroCb in there?

On these days ordinary thunderstorms were over or beside the fire, so the satellite test can't tell a pyroCb from the storms around it and every score on this site leaves them out. A trained eye can do better. Each day below has its GOES cloud-top panels, the radar column over the fire and the HRRR sounding at the fire front. Look for a tower that starts on the fire pixels, grows faster or colder than the storms around it, and keeps a debris-filled (low correlation) column under it.

How to review

  1. Pick a day from the list (filters narrow it; "Not yet reviewed" hides the days you have already called).
  2. Satellite panels: is the coldest top rooted on the fire pixels (circled), and did it appear there first, or did a storm drift over the fire? The onset check already flags days with 30+ cold pixels in the hour before the fire's tower.
  3. Radar: a pyroCb column rises from the fire's own echo, with low correlation coefficient (debris, under about 0.8) low down and an ice cap (high CC) aloft. A storm core over the fire has high CC all the way down and no root in the plume. The time-height chart shows when the fire's column grew.
  4. Sounding: could the fire have done it? Moist, unstable air aloft and a low PFT make a pyroCb plausible.
  5. Record your call. Calls are kept in this browser only; Export them as a CSV and send it to the site's analyst to fold them into the review (scripts/pyrocb_review_import.py).
