Soundings: the air over the fire

A sounding is a vertical profile of the atmosphere. These come from the HRRR model's hourly analysis at the fire's most active front, at the hour the fire came closest to its pyroCb threshold (highest FP/PFT; otherwise the hour of lowest PFT). They show why the same fire could make a pyroCb one afternoon and only smoke the next. How the soundings are made →

How to read them

The chart

Height runs up the page as pressure (hPa, surface to 100 hPa). Temperature runs along the bottom, but the lines of equal temperature lean up and to the right at 45° (a "skew-T"), so the profiles stand up instead of falling off the chart.

  • Red: air temperature. Blue: dewpoint. Where they come close, the air is nearly saturated.
  • Purple: the mixed layer, the deep, well-stirred layer the fire's heat goes into: its temperature (solid, following a dry adiabat) and moisture (dotted), meeting at its cloud base (the dot, "ML-LCL").
  • Navy: the saturation-point curve: where a plume cloud would form if the fire adds more and more heat (each 15 K of extra warmth comes with about 1 g/kg of extra moisture from burning).
  • Brown: the pyroCb parcel. It rises dry (dashed) to the star, zfc, the lowest point on the navy curve from which it stays warmer than the air all the way to −20 °C, then follows the solid brown line up to where it stops being buoyant (the parcel EL line).
  • Green dash-dot: the downdraft parcel behind DCAPE. It starts in the driest air of the lowest 400 hPa and sinks to the ground, kept saturated by evaporating rain or ice.
  • Dashed lines mark the cloud base, zfc, the parcel's equilibrium level and the −20 °C level. Wind barbs are on the right.

The box

  • PFT: the least fire power (GW) that can push a plume to zfc against the wind and still have it convect, from zfc, the extra warmth needed (Δθfc) and the mixed-layer wind (UML). "No pyroCb solution" means no amount of fire power gets there.
  • PCAPE: the parcel's buoyant energy above zfc (J/kg), the area between the brown line and the red one.
  • DCAPE: how much energy that sinking parcel gains on the way down (J/kg), the area between the green line and the red one. More DCAPE means stronger possible downdrafts and gusty outflow at the fire.
  • Then the mixed layer's moisture and depth, and what the fire did that day: FP/PFT, peak fire power, the coldest cloud top GOES saw rising from the fire, and the growth.

What to look for

  • PyroCb days: moist air aloft (blue close to red around 500–300 hPa, often monsoon moisture), a mixed layer that reaches its cloud base, a low star and a low PFT that the fire's power could match.
  • No-deep-cloud days: very dry air (blue far to the left), a deep hot mixed layer whose cloud base sits too high, and often no pyroCb solution at all, even on the biggest wind-driven runs.
