Methods & sources

How every number on this site was made, what it can't tell you, and where the data came from.

Fires and fire days

Every California and Hawaii fire of 1,000 acres or more since 2017 (the census below). A fire day runs 06:00 to 06:00 local time. Days were kept when satellite-mapped growth reached 1,000 acres or summed VIIRS/MODIS fire radiative power reached 3,000 MW: 1,736 days in all.

Growth is the area of VIIRS/MODIS active-fire detections inside the final MTBS perimeter (or the NIFC WFIGS perimeter for fires MTBS hasn't mapped yet), dated to the fire day.

How it was built. The work started in October 2026 on 23 large fires picked for towering plumes or big wind-driven runs (Dixie, Carr, Loyalton, North Complex, August Complex, Creek, Caldor, Ranch, Camp, LNU, Sugar, Antelope, McKinney, Mosquito, Park, Ferguson, Woolsey, Castle, Bobcat, York, Borel, Bridge and Line). The census then ran every other fire of that size through the same pipeline. Everything on this site now comes from all of them together. Those 23 fires still carry a few extra layers (see the last point below).

The fire census: every fire of 1,000 acres or more, 2017–2026

We looked at all fires from 2026 back to 2017 that burned 1,000 acres or more in California and Hawaii. Every fire of that size runs through the same pipeline and is classed by the same rules.

  • The fire list comes from the toolkit's satellite growth sets (MTBS perimeters, and NIFC WFIGS perimeters for fires MTBS hasn't mapped yet), taking every event with a perimeter of 1,000 acres or more.
  • Start dates follow the data that identify a pyroCb. California starts on 24 May 2017, the first GOES-16 fire product on the public archive. Hawaii starts on 27 Aug 2018, the first GOES-17 fire product; GOES-East can't see Hawaii. Before these dates there are no 5–10-minute fire pixels and cloud tops to tie a cloud to a fire.
  • Hawaii uses ERA5 reanalysis columns (3-hourly) for the PFT instead of HRRR, which doesn't cover Hawaii, and local Hawaii time for the 11 a.m.–11 p.m. window.
  • Completeness check. Every fire record of 1,000 acres or more in the fire-occurrence database (FPA-FOD and its 2021+ update) that isn't in a growth event was explained: a member of a complex, a duplicate record, before the start date, or without any perimeter or satellite detections to back the acreage. Nine fires that had a real perimeter of 1,000+ acres but were missed by the growth sets were added.
  • Review. Every day the classifier called pyroCb was checked by eye against the GOES panels, the fire's power and the radar. Away from the best-known fires the classifier mostly fires on ordinary thunderstorms. The review kept 15 pyroCb days (including Caldwell/July Complex 23 Jul 2020 and Lava 30 Jun 2021); the rest are listed as ordinary storms over the fire. Meteorologists can review those days on Storms over fires.
  • Onset check: a day is ordinary storms over the fire, not pyroCb, when 30 or more unrooted −40 °C pixels were already present in the hour before the first fire-rooted −40 °C scan.
  • Radar ran on every candidate day (pyroCb, storms over fire, pyroCu) with a WSR-88D within 200 km; KHNX and KDAX are excluded from the scores (Sierra terrain clutter). A fire-rooted echo top of 10.8 km or more also appears under ordinary storm cores and all-day cloud decks, so radar confirms a pyroCb only when GOES shows an isolated fire-rooted tower.
  • Archive gaps. GOES-16 sent nothing from 30 Nov to 13 Dec 2017 (its move east), and the GOES-18 fire product is missing for about 6–25 Nov 2025. Days with fewer than six fire scans are listed as "no GOES scans" and left out of the scores.
  • Layers only some fires carry: LANDFIRE and FOFEM fuels, PSA NFDRS indices and field live fuel moisture exist only for the 23 fires the work started with, and OMPS aerosol only for some fires. Those factors are scored on the fires that have them; their scores rest on far fewer days.

What the plume did: the four classes

Every fire day gets one class from the GOES-West 10.3 µm cloud-top temperature (GOES-16 for the 2018 fires, before GOES-17 became GOES-West; GOES-17 for 2019–2022; GOES-18 from 2023; GOES-16 also fills GOES-17 archive gaps). The scans used are every 10 minutes from 11:00 to 23:00 PDT. In each scan, a cloud counts as rooted on the fire when its connected area of cold pixels reaches within 20 km of a GOES fire pixel from the past hour, after correcting the cloud's position for parallax (a tall cloud appears displaced away from the satellite).

ClassRuleWhat it means
pyroCbA fire-rooted cloud colder than −40 °C in at least two scans, with no other cloud that cold within 100 km.The plume made a thunderstorm-depth cloud: below about −40 °C cloud water freezes almost completely (the top is glaciated), roughly 9–10 km up in a California summer.
pyroCuA fire-rooted cloud colder than −20 °C in at least two scans, but not a pyroCb.The plume made a deep cumulus cap, roughly 7–9 km up, that stopped short of a thunderstorm.
ordinary storms over fireThe cold tops near the fire belong to a wide cloud shield (over 1,500 connected pixels below −20 °C with more cold cloud around it), or other −40 °C clouds sit within 100 km of the fire, or a reviewer saw ordinary storms over or beside the fire.A pyroCb could be hiding inside, and satellite can't tell. These days are left out of every score (76 days).
no deep plume cloudEverything else: fewer than two scans with a fire-rooted cloud colder than −20 °C, and no reviewer finding of one.The fire's plume stayed shallow, warmer than −20 °C at the top, or was smoke alone. It is not "no plume" and not "a quiet day": many of the biggest runs are in this class.

Fire power and the pyroCb firepower threshold

Fire power (FP): GOES fire radiative power within the final fire perimeter (plus a ~3 km buffer), hourly, × 3.9 to convert radiated to convective heat (Tory & Kepert 2021). GOES-16 values used to fill GOES-17 gaps were scaled × 1.5, from the median ratio where both satellites saw the fire.

PFT: the pyroCb firepower threshold of Tory & Kepert (2021), computed from hourly HRRR analysis soundings at the day's most active fire front, 12–22 PDT. It finds the smallest fire power for which a plume, mixed with the entrainment-weighted mixed layer, reaches free convection and rises to the −20 °C level. When no plume can do that, PFT has no solution.

PFT = 397.3 · ρ₀ · zfc² · UML · Δθfc  (≈ 0.3 · z² · U · Δθ GW, z in km)

FP/PFT compares the two in the same hour; the day's maximum is used.

Pyroconvective CAPE (PCAPE): the buoyant energy of that same pyroCb parcel, the area between its path and the environment from zfc up to its equilibrium level, Rd∫(Tv,parcel − Tv,env) d ln p, in J/kg. Days with no PFT solution have PCAPE 0. HRRR columns run from the surface to 100 hPa, above every pyroCb parcel's equilibrium level in the study.

Other inputs

  • Radar: NEXRAD Level II, the tallest echo column rooted on the fire, every candidate day 12–23 PDT (see Radar). A radar is left out when more than a quarter of its fire's no-plume days read a column of 10.8 km or more: terrain clutter for the Hanford (KHNX: Creek, Ferguson) and Sacramento (KDAX: Caldor, Mosquito) radars, and long-range beam blur for Beale (KBBX) on the Ranch Fire.
  • Fuels and terrain: LANDFIRE, matched to before each fire: the newest fuel vintage from before the fire year (LF 2016 for 2018–2022 fires, LF 2022 for 2023, LF 2023 for 2024) with every later LANDFIRE disturbance layer up to the year before the fire masked in, and LF 2020 terrain. Fuel-based fire power = 0.7 × 15 MJ/kg × available fuel × area burned ÷ burning time (from GOES timing).
  • FCCS fuel and consumption (compared side by side with the above): LANDFIRE FCCS fuelbeds (LF2016; LF2023 for 2024 fires) run through FOFEM (SpatialFOFEM, Burnup for litter and wood, duff from 3 × 1000-h moisture) at each day's gridMET 100-h and 1000-h fuel moisture; FCCS fuel fire power = 0.7 × 15 MJ/kg × consumed surface fuel × area ÷ burning time. See the Factor guide for the pros and cons of each.
  • Fuel moisture: NFDRS fuel model Y values (ERC, 100-h, 1000-h, KBDI) averaged over the RAWS of the fire's Predictive Services Area, with each day's ERC and 1000-h as percentiles of that PSA's full-year record (March 2005 to 2025, every day of the year: the fire danger charts' percentile basis); gridMET 100-h and 1000-h at the fire; and measured live fuel moisture from the National Fuel Moisture Database within 60 km (values outside 30–400% dropped).
  • Weather regimes: each day's ERA5 500 hPa height anomaly matched to the six regimes of the AI ensemble research viewer; ERA5 700 hPa moisture at the fire; documented Northern California critical fire-weather patterns.
  • Forecast hindcast: HRRR 12Z run, forecast hours 9–15, at the previous day's fire front.
  • OMPS aerosol index: the 0–50 "pyrocumulonimbus" UV Aerosol Index layer that FIRMS shows, read from NASA GIBS. It is the peak within 150 km of the fire front on the fire day's ~13:30 pass, and within 500 km on the next day's pass. The 2017–2024 fires use Suomi NPP (50 km pixels); a separate 2025–26 test used NOAA-20 (17 km) and NOAA-21 (12 km). See Satellite.

Scoring

For each factor, the cut that best separates pyroCb days from pyroCu and no-plume days is chosen by balanced accuracy (the mean of the hit rate and the correct-rejection rate). Leave-one-fire-out scores choose the cut with one fire held out, test it on that fire, and sum over every fire in turn. These are the honest out-of-sample numbers, and the ones shown as "each fire held out".

Caveats

    • HRRR has known biases in boundary-layer moisture and depth; PFT is sensitive to both.
    • None of this is an operational forecast product.
